What Is the Difference Between an Orthopaedician and a Physiotherapist?

An orthopaedician assesses musculoskeletal conditions and can investigate injuries or conditions that need specialist management. A physiotherapist assesses movement and function and helps people manage suitable conditions through rehabilitation.

When Can Physiotherapy Be a Practical Starting Point?

A physiotherapy assessment may be appropriate when lower back pain affects bending, sitting, standing, walking, work or exercise and there are no warning signs requiring urgent medical care. The physiotherapist considers symptoms, medical history and function, then advises whether rehabilitation is suitable or further medical assessment is needed.

When Should You Consult an Orthopaedician?

Medical assessment may be appropriate when pain follows significant injury, is severe or progressively worsening, or requires further investigation. New neurological symptoms or unexplained concerning symptoms also warrant assessment. The appropriate specialist depends on symptoms and suspected cause.

Can You Benefit From Both Physiotherapy and Specialist Care?

Yes. An orthopaedician may investigate a condition while physiotherapy supports appropriate movement and rehabilitation. Rehabilitation after surgery may be coordinated with the surgical team. Physiotherapy should not delay necessary medical assessment.

Can Physiotherapy Help With a Slipped Disc?

Some people with disc-related symptoms improve with appropriate non-surgical management, which may include physiotherapy. An MRI finding alone does not determine symptom severity or the treatment needed. Physiotherapy cannot guarantee that a disc will return to a particular position or that surgery will never be needed.

What If Your Lower Back Pain Travels Down Your Leg?

Pain travelling from the back into the buttock or leg may be associated with sciatica or another condition. Assessment may consider pain pattern, tingling, numbness, strength and whether further investigation is needed.

Seek emergency medical care for back pain accompanied by new bladder or bowel dysfunction, numbness around the groin or saddle area, or rapidly worsening neurological symptoms.

Do You Need an MRI Before Starting Physiotherapy?

Not necessarily. Imaging is not routinely needed for uncomplicated lower back pain. A healthcare professional should consider it when there are specific concerns or when results are likely to change management.

What Can Physiotherapy Involve?

When appropriate, a plan may include movement and functional assessment, individualised exercise, guidance on daily activities, selected manual therapy as part of a broader plan, and progress monitoring. Treatment should be tailored to the person; there is no guaranteed recovery timeline or fixed number of sessions for every case.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I consult an orthopaedician or physiotherapist for lower back pain?

For many uncomplicated, movement-related cases, physiotherapy assessment can be a practical starting point. Specialist assessment may be appropriate when symptoms suggest an injury or a condition requiring investigation. Some people benefit from both.

Can physiotherapy help lower back pain without surgery?

Physiotherapy can help manage suitable cases through individualised exercise, movement guidance and other appropriate interventions.

Does every case of lower back pain require an MRI?

No. Imaging is not routinely needed for every episode of uncomplicated lower back pain.

Can a slipped disc improve with physiotherapy?

Some people improve with non-surgical management, which may include physiotherapy. The approach depends on symptoms and assessment findings.

What if lower back pain travels down my leg?

Seek assessment to identify the likely cause. Emergency warning signs include new bladder or bowel dysfunction, saddle-area numbness or rapidly worsening weakness.

Is rest the best treatment?

Prolonged bed rest is generally not recommended for uncomplicated lower back pain. Appropriate activity and gradual return to normal routines are often encouraged.

Can physiotherapy and orthopaedic care happen together?

Yes. Physiotherapy may complement investigation, medical management or post-operative rehabilitation.

How long does physiotherapy take?

Recovery varies with the cause, symptom duration, health factors, functional limitations and response to treatment.

Should I continue physiotherapy if pain is worsening?

Worsening symptoms should be reassessed. Seek urgent care for serious warning signs.

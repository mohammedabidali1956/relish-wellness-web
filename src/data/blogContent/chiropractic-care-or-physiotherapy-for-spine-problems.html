Having spine pain? Physiotherapy may help—and sometimes, you may need both physiotherapy and specialist care.

Neck pain, back pain or pain travelling into your arms or legs can leave you wondering: Should I try chiropractic care, consult a physiotherapist, or see a specialist first?

The answer depends on what's causing your symptoms. For many suitable spine problems, a physiotherapy assessment is a practical starting point. A qualified physiotherapist can assess your movement, strength and functional limitations, recommend an appropriate rehabilitation plan, and identify when further medical assessment is needed.

These options do not always have to be an either-or decision. Depending on your condition, physiotherapy may form part of your care alongside an orthopaedician, neurologist or another appropriate specialist.

The goal is to understand your problem, receive the right care and work towards returning to everyday activities.

Chiropractic Care and Physiotherapy: What's the Difference?

Chiropractic care commonly includes hands-on approaches, including spinal manipulation, depending on the practitioner's training and approach. Physiotherapy is a broader rehabilitation profession that may include assessment, exercise, movement advice, selected manual therapy and functional rehabilitation.

Ask any practitioner what the assessment involves, what treatment is proposed, what risks and alternatives exist, and how progress will be evaluated.

How Can Physiotherapy Help Spine Problems?

Depending on assessment findings, physiotherapy may help with lower back pain, neck pain, movement limitations, reduced strength or tolerance for everyday activities, and rehabilitation following medical treatment or surgery when cleared by the treating team.

A tailored plan may include exercise, movement guidance, education and selected manual therapy. No single technique suits everyone, and results cannot be guaranteed.

Is Spinal Manipulation Necessary?

No. Spinal manipulation is not necessary for everyone with spine pain. If it is proposed, ask about expected benefits, alternatives, risks and whether your symptoms or medical history create concerns. High-velocity neck manipulation is not suitable for everyone.

When Should You Seek Medical Assessment?

Seek urgent or emergency assessment for new bladder or bowel dysfunction with back pain, numbness around the groin or saddle area, rapidly worsening weakness, significant trauma, or other serious warning signs. Persistent, progressive or unexplained symptoms also warrant medical review.

Do You Need an MRI Before Physiotherapy?

Not necessarily. Imaging is not routinely required for every uncomplicated episode of neck or lower back pain. A healthcare professional should consider imaging when the history and examination suggest it is needed or when results are likely to change management.

How Can You Choose a Practitioner?

Ask what the assessment suggests, what the treatment goals are, what benefits and risks exist, how progress will be monitored, and what symptoms should prompt referral. Choose a qualified professional who explains the plan clearly and refers when appropriate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is physiotherapy or chiropractic care better for back pain?

It depends on the diagnosis, symptoms, preferences and proposed treatment. Physiotherapy can offer assessment-led rehabilitation that may include exercise, advice and selected hands-on techniques.

Can physiotherapy help neck pain?

Physiotherapy can help suitable patients manage neck pain and improve function. New or progressive neurological symptoms need medical assessment.

Does spinal manipulation fix a slipped disc?

No treatment should be described as guaranteeing that a disc returns to a particular position. Care should be based on symptoms, assessment and evidence-informed options.

Do all spine problems need an orthopaedician?

No. Some people can begin with physiotherapy assessment, while others need medical or specialist assessment first.

When is spine pain an emergency?

Back pain with new bladder or bowel dysfunction, saddle-area numbness or rapidly worsening weakness requires emergency medical assessment.
